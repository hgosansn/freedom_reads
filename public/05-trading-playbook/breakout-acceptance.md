# Breakout Acceptance

This setup participates when the market leaves balance and demonstrates that the new area can support trade. The edge comes from acceptance, not from crossing the line first.

## Conditions

- A mature, clearly bounded balance
- A break with displacement and participation
- Time, closes, or volume building outside
- Value or developing POC beginning to migrate
- A retest that holds, or continued compression above the boundary

## Entry models

**Retest:** Enter when the old boundary holds from the outside and lower-timeframe structure resumes. This offers tighter risk but may not occur.

**Acceptance continuation:** Enter after consolidation outside resolves in the breakout direction. This reduces missed trades but usually requires wider structural risk.

**Initial break:** Reserved for tested momentum models with predefined slippage and event rules; it has the greatest false-break exposure.

## Invalidation and targets

Invalidation is sustained return into the old balance, not ordinary testing of its edge. Targets may use measured range expansion, prior external liquidity, composite nodes, or volatility-based projections. Reduce expectations if the breakout runs directly into higher-timeframe supply or demand.

{% hint style="success" %}
The cleanest accepted breakout changes the market's behavior: the former edge stops acting as an extreme and begins acting as a base.
{% endhint %}

## No-trade conditions

Skip thin, isolated venue spikes; breaks without participation; entries already far from invalidation; or breakouts into an immediate higher-timeframe barrier.

**Drill:** Backtest one acceptance definition unchanged over at least 50 examples. Track retest frequency, false-break rate, and excursion before continuation.
