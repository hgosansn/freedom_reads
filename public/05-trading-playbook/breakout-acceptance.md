# Breakout Acceptance

The Breakout Acceptance setup enters after the market leaves balance and establishes trade in the new area. Crossing the boundary identifies an attempt; acceptance qualifies the trade.

## Conditions

- A mature, clearly bounded balance
- A break with displacement and participation
- Time, closes, or volume building outside
- Value or developing POC beginning to migrate
- A retest that holds, or continued compression above the boundary

## Entry models

**Retest:** Enter when the old boundary holds from the outside and lower-timeframe structure resumes. This provides a closer structural invalidation but creates non-fill risk.

**Acceptance continuation:** Enter after consolidation outside resolves in the breakout direction. This captures breaks that never retest at the cost of wider structural risk.

**Initial break:** Reserved for tested momentum models with predefined slippage and event rules; it has the greatest false-break exposure.

## Invalidation and targets

Invalidation is sustained return into the old balance. A test of the edge remains valid while trade continues to develop outside. Derive targets from external liquidity, composite nodes, or a tested range projection. A higher-timeframe opposing distribution directly above the break reduces available payoff.

{% hint style="success" %}
The cleanest accepted breakout changes the market's behavior: the former edge stops acting as an extreme and begins acting as a base.
{% endhint %}

## No-trade conditions

Skip thin, isolated venue spikes; breaks without participation; entries already far from invalidation; or breakouts into an immediate higher-timeframe barrier.

**Validation sample:** Test one acceptance definition unchanged across at least 100 breaks, with 30 reserved out of sample. Track retest frequency, false-break rate, MAE, MFE, slippage, and excursion before continuation.
