# Acceptance / rejection

Acceptance means the market is willing to conduct sustained two-sided trade at a price. Rejection means the auction tests an area and rapidly moves away. These are processes, not single-candle patterns.

## Evidence of acceptance

- Time spent beyond the reference
- Multiple closes and rotations outside it
- Volume building in the new area
- A retest that holds from the opposite side
- The point of control or developing value migrating toward price

## Evidence of rejection

- Fast excursion followed by fast return
- Excess or a pronounced tail at the extreme
- Aggressive flow that fails to advance price
- Re-entry into the prior range or value area
- Follow-through away from the rejected area

Time matters. On a one-minute execution chart, several closes may look convincing while the hourly auction remains unresolved. Match acceptance criteria to the setup horizon.

## The close-location trap

A close beyond a level is useful but insufficient. Thin weekend books, liquidation spikes, and event volatility can create dramatic closes without durable participation. Look for the market's ability to revisit and defend the new area.

{% hint style="success" %}
Define acceptance before the test—for example, “two 15-minute closes above, volume building above, and a retest that does not close back inside.”
{% endhint %}
