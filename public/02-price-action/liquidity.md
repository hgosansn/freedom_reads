# Liquidity

Liquidity is the market's capacity to transact without excessive price impact. Traders often use the word for visible areas where orders are likely concentrated: prior highs and lows, equal extremes, range edges, liquidation clusters, and high-volume reference points.

## Why price seeks liquidity

Large participants need counterparties. Stops above highs become buy orders; stops below lows become sell orders. Breakout traders add flow in the same places. These concentrations can facilitate continuation or provide the fuel for reversal.

The level does not predict the outcome. Observe what happens after it trades:

- **Sweep and reject:** price crosses, cannot build trade, and returns through the level.
- **Consume and accept:** price crosses, holds, retests, and continues.
- **Repeated test:** each visit may consume resting liquidity, but it can also advertise defended inventory. Response decides.

## Crypto considerations

Liquidation heatmaps estimate leveraged exposure; they are not a complete order book. Visible depth can be canceled, spoofed, or fragmented across exchanges. Use these tools to frame likely interaction zones, then validate with traded volume and price response.

{% hint style="danger" %}
Never place risk solely because a heatmap shows a bright band. Estimates can be stale, model-dependent, and vulnerable to abrupt positioning changes.
{% endhint %}

**Drill:** Mark prior-day high/low, equal highs/lows, and range edges before 20 sessions. Record which were untouched, swept, or accepted beyond. Measure the maximum excursion after each outcome.
